# /tck-hiring-loop

Use the posting and the CV already supplied in the conversation or as files. Accept a posting URL. If an input is missing or inaccessible, ask only for the missing material. Use the conversation language from learner/conventions.md unless the candidate explicitly requests another interview language.

Briefly introduce the simulated process. Use the posting's interview descriptions, named roles, seniority and exercise format first. Fill missing details from the employer and role, marking assumptions. If the published sequence differs from the three-round practice sequence below, explain the difference and map its roles and exercises to the closest rounds. Keep the job requirements and the candidate's claims distinct.

1. **Recruiter, Talent Acquisition or HR.** Explore motivation for this particular role, relevant experience and practical fit. Ask for a specific contribution from the CV. Adapt the discussion to the employer, for example client assignments at an ESN or product ownership at a software publisher.
2. **Hiring manager, discipline lead or senior practitioner.** Select a representative problem from the job. Start from a relevant CV claim and probe the candidate's individual decisions. Follow the advertised role-specific format where available. Match the level of difficulty and any rules about documentation or AI tools to the process.
3. **CEO, founder or HR.** Choose the interviewer from the process or employer context. Explore judgment, collaboration and motivation through a concrete decision the candidate has faced. Give the candidate space to ask the interviewer a question.

Start in character with one question and wait. Ask one question per turn, using neutral follow-ups to clarify evidence. Give feedback after the round. Carry forward the facts and unresolved questions from previous rounds.

At each gate, give a simulated pass or fail against the role's requirements, citing specific answers and their impact. If evidence is insufficient, ask a targeted follow-up before deciding. Advance only on a pass. On failure, stop the process, give one priority for practice and let the candidate retry that round with a fresh question. After the final round, summarize the three decisions, the most persuasive evidence and one next practice task. Keep claims about hiring decisions explicitly within the simulation.
